Where it all started
The story of rock'n'roll
Rock'n'roll grew up in the United States in the late 1940s and early 1950s, born from blues, jump blues, jazz and gospel with a good measure of Western swing and country thrown in. Piano and saxophone led the early records. By the middle of the decade the electric guitar had taken over, with a string bass and a snare hitting the backbeat.
It was more than a sound. It changed how people dressed, talked and danced, and it put teenagers of every background in front of the same radio. That first wave, from the 1950s into the early 1960s, is the music Splinterwood plays all day and all night.
1934
The Boswell Sisters perform "Rock and Roll" in the film Transatlantic Merry-Go-Round.
1942
Billboard columnist Maurie Orodenker starts describing upbeat records as rock-and-roll, among them Sister Rosetta Tharpe's "Rock Me".
1943
The Rock and Roll Inn is open for business as a music venue in South Merchantville, New Jersey.
1951
Cleveland disc jockey Alan Freed plays the music on air and spreads the phrase.
